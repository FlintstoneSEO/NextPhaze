# NextPhaze Art Directions

## Direction A: Championship Editorial

An asymmetric sports-publication system with large cropped photography, editorial pacing, and compact proof statistics. Career coverage would appear as documented secondary modules after the offer. This direction has strong authority but depends heavily on rights-cleared historical media and risks making Carrington's career visually dominant.

Business fit: 8/10. Media risk: high. Conversion fit: high.

## Direction B: Training Lab

A precise athlete-development system based on repetition, movement, route geometry, and field notation. Pricing and session choices sit in strong modular structures. Original CSS markings and restrained micro-interactions communicate process without inventing metrics.

Typography uses a condensed display face with a neutral, highly readable sans-serif body. The palette locks to off-black, silver, warm white, and one metallic-gold accent. Layouts are asymmetric on desktop and become strict single-column compositions below 768px. Interactive elements use sharp corners, strong borders, visible focus states, and tactile active feedback.

Business fit: 10/10. Media risk: low. Conversion fit: very high.

## Direction C: Journey / Next Phase

A progression-led system that maps training to movement from current ability toward the next phase. Carrington's Houston-to-Northwood-to-WMU-to-Ironmen story becomes supporting proof. The direction fits the name naturally, but its pathway language can become generic or biography-heavy.

Business fit: 8/10. Media risk: medium. Conversion fit: high.

## Approved selection

Training Lab was approved by the client on 2026-08-30. It best supports the training-first hierarchy, transparent pricing, limited confirmed media rights, accessibility, and performance. Championship Editorial may influence the coach page's typography and image cropping, but will not replace Training Lab's structure.
